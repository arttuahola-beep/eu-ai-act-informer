The AI Act follows your customers, not your servers

The AI Act's reach depends on where an AI system is used and where its output lands. It does not depend on where the code runs or where the vendor sits.

Article 2(1) sets the scope. Four routes matter for an online shop.

First, an EU retailer that uses an AI system is a deployer under Art. 2(1)(b). A US vendor does not change that. Nor do servers outside the EU.

Second, the vendor is caught too. Art. 2(1)(a) covers providers that place AI systems on the EU market or put them into service here. Where they are established does not matter.

Third, a shop based outside the EU can be caught. Art. 2(1)(c) covers providers and deployers in third countries when the AI system's output is used in the Union. A UK or US webshop running an AI shopping assistant for EU customers should plan on that basis. Recital 22 gives the reason: to stop the rules being avoided by moving processing abroad.

Fourth, importers and distributors of AI systems are in scope under Art. 2(1)(d).

What does this mean in practice? The Article 50(1) duty to tell people they are talking to an AI has applied since 2 August 2026. It covers these setups now.

High-risk AI is rarer in retail, but it happens. A non-EU provider of a high-risk system must appoint an EU authorised representative by written mandate before making it available here (Art. 22(1)). Annex III high-risk duties apply from 2 December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

There are limits. Purely personal, non-professional use is outside the Act (Art. 2(10)). Research and testing before market launch is outside too, but real-world testing is not (Art. 2(8)). The Act does not replace consumer protection law or the GDPR (Art. 2(7) and 2(9)).

Best practice: in your AI inventory, record where each tool's output is used, not just where the vendor is based. For any high-risk system from a non-EU vendor, ask who their EU authorised representative will be. Put the answer in the contract.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 2, 22 and 50 and Recital 22; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus).
