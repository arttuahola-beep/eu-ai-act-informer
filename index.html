One key takeaway from the EU AI Act, every weekday.

Friday 30 October 2026 Online retail

Customers can complain and staff are protected, so give AI concerns a door inside first

Anyone can now report a suspected AI Act breach to a regulator. Staff who report breaches are protected as whistleblowers. An online retailer should open its own reporting route for AI concerns before people go straight to the authority.

Customers can complain directly. Article 85 lets any person or company file a complaint with the market surveillance authority. They only need grounds to think the AI Act was broken. The authority must take complaints into account in its market surveillance work. Other legal remedies stay open.

Consumer groups can act too. Article 110 adds the AI Act to Annex I of Directive (EU) 2020/1828. That is the EU law on representative actions. Qualified consumer bodies can bring collective cases for AI Act breaches that harm consumers.

Staff reports are protected. Article 87 applies the EU Whistleblower Directive, (EU) 2019/1937, to AI Act breaches. Recital 172 gives the reason: people who report these breaches should be protected under Union law. The directive bans retaliation in Article 19. Dismissal, demotion and intimidation are on its list.

Your existing hotline must cover AI. Under Article 8 of the directive, private firms with 50 or more workers must run internal reporting channels. Under Article 9, you must confirm receipt within seven days. You must give feedback within three months. Reports must stay confidential.

Both rules already apply. Articles 85 and 87 have applied since 2 August 2026. The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, delayed Annex III high-risk duties to 2 December 2027. It did not delay these routes. Complaints can already target live duties, such as the Article 50 chatbot notice and the Article 5 bans on manipulative design.

What a retailer can do now: - Add "AI Act" as a named category in your whistleblowing channel. - Tell staff who build or run pricing, recommendation and chatbot tools that they can use it. - Give customers a clear way to raise concerns about AI features, such as a contact form on your AI notice page. - Route AI complaints to someone who can check them against your AI inventory. - Keep a record of each concern and what you did about it.

A concern fixed inside the company is cheaper than one investigated by a regulator.

General information, not legal advice.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 85, 87 and 110, Recital 172; Directive (EU) 2019/1937, Articles 8, 9 and 19.
