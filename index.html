Tuesday 17 November 2026 Online retail

Product recommendations and personalised prices are not high-risk AI, but they are not rule-free

Takeaway: Under the EU AI Act, product recommendations and personalised pricing in an online shop are not high-risk AI. Annex III does not list them. But the bans in Article 5, the chatbot rule in Article 50, and EU consumer and data law still apply.

Why they are not high-risk. An AI system is high-risk if it is a safety part of a product covered by Annex I (Article 6(1)), or if its use is listed in Annex III (Article 6(2)). Annex III covers areas such as biometrics, jobs and workers, credit scoring, and life and health insurance pricing. Shopping recommendations and retail price-setting are not on that list. So the heavy high-risk duties do not apply to them. That means no Article 9 to 15 requirements for the provider and no Article 26 duties for the shop as deployer. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) moved Annex III duties to 2 December 2027, but that date does not matter for these tools.

Watch the edge. The answer changes if the tool starts deciding who gets credit. A pay-later or invoice check at checkout that scores a shopper's creditworthiness is high-risk under Annex III point 5(b).

What still applies.

Article 5 bans (since 2 February 2025). Article 5(1)(a) bans manipulative or deceptive techniques that distort behaviour and cause significant harm. Article 5(1)(b) bans exploiting weakness linked to age, disability, or social or economic situation. A pricing model that pushes higher prices onto people in financial trouble is the kind of risk to test for. Breaches sit in the top fine tier: up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover (Article 99(3)). Article 50(1) (since 2 August 2026). If an AI assistant talks to shoppers, it must be clear to them that they are dealing with AI, unless that is obvious. Consumer law. The AI Act leaves consumer protection law untouched (Article 2(9)). The Consumer Rights Directive requires a shop to tell consumers, before they buy, when a price was personalised on the basis of automated decision-making (Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 6(1)(ea), added by Directive (EU) 2019/2161). Recital 45 of Directive 2019/2161 says this does not cover dynamic or real-time pricing that reacts to market demand without personalisation. Data protection. Personalisation runs on personal data. The GDPR still requires a lawful basis for that profiling. Article 22 GDPR adds limits where a solely automated decision has legal or similarly significant effects.

Best practice. Keep a short AI register. For each recommender or pricing tool, record "not high-risk" and the reason: not listed in Annex III and no credit decision. Test pricing outcomes for groups that may be vulnerable. Show a clear "personalised price" notice where one applies. Re-check the classification whenever the tool gains a new purpose.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 2(9), 5(1)(a)-(b), 6, 50(1), 99(3), 113 and Annex III; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus); Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 6(1)(ea); Directive (EU) 2019/2161, Recital 45; Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Article 22.
