Friday 6 November 2026 Financial institutions

In-house credit scoring needs an EU database filing before go-live

If your bank builds a credit-scoring model and puts it into service under your own name, you must register yourself and that system in the EU AI database before go-live.

Credit scoring sits in Annex III point 5(b) of the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Article 49(1) says the provider, or its authorised representative, registers in the EU database under Article 71. Article 16(i) makes that a provider duty. Putting a model into service for your own lending counts.

The filing is public for this use case. Article 71(4) makes most Article 49 entries open. Annex VIII Section A lists what you submit: who you are, the system name and ID, intended purpose, a short note on inputs and operating logic, status, Member States, the EU declaration of conformity, and electronic instructions for use. Keep the entry up to date.

Two limits matter. Article 49(3) makes deployer registration a duty for public authorities. A private bank that only buys a vendor scorecard does not file under Article 49(3). And if you claim an Article 6(3) "not high-risk" exit, Article 49(2) still requires registration. The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, kept that rule and only cut a few Annex VIII Section B fields.

Annex III provider duties, including Article 16(i) registration, apply from 2 December 2027. What to do now: list every in-house credit or insurance-pricing model you put into service under your name. Assign an owner for the Annex VIII pack. Treat registration as a go-live gate next to conformity assessment, not an afterthought.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 16(i), 49 and 71 and Annex VIII; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus), Recital 22 and Article 113.
