Friday 23 October 2026 Financial institutions

Only life and health insurance pricing is on the AI Act's high-risk list

For insurers, the AI Act's high-risk list names just one insurance use. It is AI for risk assessment and pricing of individuals in life and health insurance. Motor, home and travel pricing tools are not on that list.

The exact wording matters. Annex III point 5(c) covers "AI systems intended to be used for risk assessment and pricing in relation to natural persons in the case of life and health insurance" (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). It is about individuals. It is about two lines of business only.

Why these two? Recital 58 explains it. Life and health pricing can shape people's livelihoods. Badly built systems can lead to financial exclusion and discrimination.

Recital 58 also names uses that should not count as high-risk. One is AI provided for by Union law to detect fraud in financial services. Another is AI used for prudential purposes to calculate the capital requirements of banks and insurers.

The exit route in Article 6(3) will rarely help here. That article lets some Annex III systems escape high-risk status if they pose no significant risk. But a system that profiles individuals is always high-risk. Pricing a person's life or health cover usually means profiling them.

If a model is in scope, the deployer duties are real. Article 26 sets the general duties, such as human oversight and following the instructions for use. Article 27 requires a fundamental rights impact assessment for 5(c) systems before first use. Article 86 gives affected people a right to an explanation of decisions based on the system's output.

Timing is set by the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Annex III high-risk duties apply from 2 December 2027.

Off the list does not mean rule-free. Article 4 on AI literacy and Article 50 on transparency apply to all relevant AI. GDPR and insurance law also still apply to motor or home pricing models.

Best practice: sort your model inventory by line of business and by purpose. Tag every life or health model that scores or prices individuals as high-risk. For motor, home and travel models, write a short note on why they fall outside Annex III. Your supervisor may ask for that reasoning.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Annex III point 5(c), Recital 58 and Articles 4, 6(3), 26, 27, 50 and 86; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus).
