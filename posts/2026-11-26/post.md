If your bank builds its own credit-scoring AI, someone at the bank must sign a formal statement that it complies. That statement is the EU declaration of conformity. The system also needs a CE marking.

**Why the bank?** A bank that develops a credit-scoring system and uses it under its own name is the provider (Art. 3(3)). Credit scoring of individuals is high-risk under Annex III point 5(b). Providers must draw up the declaration and affix the CE marking (Art. 16(g)–(h)).

**When?** For Annex III systems, these duties apply from 2 December 2027. That date comes from the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

**What is the declaration?** It is a written document. It can be machine-readable, physical or electronically signed (Art. 47(1)). It states that the system meets the high-risk requirements in Chapter III, Section 2 (Art. 47(2)). By signing it, the bank takes responsibility for that compliance (Art. 47(4)).

**What goes in it?** Annex V lists the content:

- the system's name, type and a unique reference for traceability;

- the provider's name and address;

- a statement that it is issued under the provider's sole responsibility;

- a statement that the system conforms with the AI Act;

- if personal data is processed, a statement that the system complies with the GDPR. For credit scoring, it almost always is;

- references to any harmonised standards or common specifications used;

- the place and date of issue, and the signer's name and role.

**How long do you keep it?** Ten years after the system is put into service (Art. 47(1)). Authorities can ask for a copy. Keep it up to date (Art. 47(4)).

**What about the CE marking?** A system provided digitally can carry a digital CE marking. People must be able to reach it easily from the system's interface, or through a machine-readable code or other electronic means (Art. 48(2)). If the marking can't go on the system, it goes on the packaging or accompanying documents (Art. 48(3)). Credit scoring uses internal control under Annex VI. So no notified body number follows the mark (Art. 43(2), Art. 48(4)).

**Order matters.** First, finish the conformity assessment (Art. 16(f)). Next, sign the declaration and affix the mark. Then register the system in the EU database before you put it into service (Art. 49(1)).

**What's at stake?** Breaching the provider duties in Art. 16 can bring fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Art. 99(4)(a)).

**Practical step:** Name the person who will sign now. Link each declaration to a version number in your model inventory. That way every live model version maps to a signed declaration.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Arts 16, 47, 48 and Annex V — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj
