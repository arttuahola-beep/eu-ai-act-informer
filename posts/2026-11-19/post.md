**Takeaway:** An online retailer may use AI to spot fraud in orders and payments. It may not use AI to predict which customer will commit a crime based only on profiling or personality traits.

**The rule.** Article 5(1)(d) of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) bans AI that assesses or predicts the risk that a person will commit a criminal offence. The ban applies when that prediction is based solely on profiling, or on a person's personality traits and characteristics. It has applied since 2 February 2025 (Art. 113). Breaches can cost up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Art. 99(3)).

**It is not only for police.** The Commission's guidelines on prohibited AI practices say the wording does not limit the ban to law enforcement (paras 207–209). Private firms can be caught when the law gives them public powers to fight crime. They can also be caught when they act for the police, or when a law requires them to assess crime risk.

**Normal fraud control is outside.** The guidelines say profiling customers for regular business, safety, or to protect financial interests is not covered (para 210). One example is detecting financial irregularities. The test is the aim. If crime risk comes up only as a side effect, the ban does not apply (para 211).

**Where the line sits for a webshop:**

- Fine: scoring an order on transaction signals. Examples are a billing and delivery mismatch, many cards tried quickly, an odd device, or past chargebacks on the account.

- Risky: labelling a person a "likely thief" or "likely fraudster" based on who they are, not on what happened. Linking a person to a high-crime home area also counts as scoring the person (para 213).

- Allowed: AI that helps a human assess a person's role in a crime, when that assessment already rests on objective, verifiable facts directly linked to the crime (Art. 5(1)(d), last sentence; para 214).

**Best practice:**

1. Write down each fraud model's purpose. It should be protecting payments and stock, not predicting who will commit crimes.

2. Keep inputs tied to orders, payments and accounts. Avoid inferring personality or demographics.

3. Have a person review before you block a customer or report them to the police.

4. Check vendor "shopper risk" or "loss prevention" tools against this line before you buy.

5. GDPR still applies, including Article 22 on decisions made solely by automated means.
