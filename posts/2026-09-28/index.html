Financial-services rules can cover AI Act monitoring

Banks that deploy high-risk AI, such as credit-scoring systems, get a real shortcut under Article 26 of the EU AI Act. The governance you already have under EU financial-services law can count toward the Act's monitoring and logging duties. Today's angle is for financial institutions.

As the deployer, you still have to use the system according to the provider's instructions and keep competent people overseeing it. You also have to make sure the input data you control is suitable, and report serious incidents to the provider and the relevant market surveillance authority. Article 26(5) adds a shortcut for monitoring. If you're already bound by EU financial-services rules on internal governance, arrangements, and processes, complying with those rules counts as meeting the Act's monitoring obligation. Under Article 26(6), you can also keep the system's automatically generated logs as part of the documentation you already maintain under that law.

The practical step is to map that overlap now, so you can show supervisors how your existing framework covers these duties. Then close the gaps your sectoral rules don't reach. One example is the Article 26(7) duty to inform workers' representatives and affected employees before high-risk AI is used in the workplace. These deployer duties for standalone high-risk systems now apply from 2 December 2027, which gives you time to do this mapping properly.
