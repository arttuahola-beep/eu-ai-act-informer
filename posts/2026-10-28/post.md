Most AI in an online shop is not high-risk. For that AI, the EU AI Act offers a voluntary tool: codes of conduct under Article 95.

Think of product recommenders, search ranking, demand forecasts and copywriting tools. None of these is listed in Annex III. So the strict Chapter III rules do not apply to them. The duties that do apply are lighter. Staff need AI literacy under Article 4. Chatbots and some AI content need disclosure under Article 50.

Article 95(1) asks the AI Office and Member States to encourage codes of conduct. These codes let firms apply some or all of the high-risk requirements to systems that are not high-risk. Those requirements sit in Chapter III, Section 2. They cover risk management, data quality, record-keeping, transparency, human oversight, and accuracy and robustness.

Article 95(2) goes wider. A code can apply to all AI systems, and deployers can join. It should have clear objectives and key performance indicators. Suggested topics include the EU ethics guidelines for trustworthy AI and energy use. They also include AI literacy, inclusive design, and harm to vulnerable people. Accessibility for people with disabilities and gender equality are named too.

Under Article 95(3), one company can write its own code. A trade association can also write one for its members. Civil society and academia can take part. Article 95(4) asks authorities to keep SMEs and start-ups in mind.

The codes are voluntary. Signing one does not make a system compliant with any binding rule. Not signing one carries no fine. Article 95 applies from 2 August 2026 under Article 113.

Why bother? A code gives the shop one standard for all its AI. It also prepares for the case where a tool drifts into high-risk use, for example worker monitoring under Annex III point 4. Those Annex III duties apply from 2 December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

Best practice for a retailer: pick two or three Section 2 controls, such as testing and logging, for your recommender. Set a measurable target for each. Review them once a year.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 4, 50, 95, 113 and Annex III; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.
