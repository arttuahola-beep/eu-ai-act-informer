Is your shop software even 'AI'? Check the definition first

Before you map any AI Act duty, check whether the tool is an "AI system" at all. Many shop tools are not. Some clearly are.

The test is in Article 3(1) of the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. An AI system is machine-based. It works with some level of autonomy. It may adapt after deployment. Above all, it infers from its input how to produce outputs. Those outputs are predictions, content, recommendations or decisions that can affect a physical or virtual environment.

The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, did not change this definition. It amended a nearby one, "safety component", and added definitions of SMEs and small mid-caps.

Inference is the key word. Recital 12 says it goes beyond basic data processing by enabling learning, reasoning or modelling. Systems built only on rules written by people are not covered.

The Commission's guidelines on the definition give examples that fit a webshop. A database query such as "all customers who bought this product last month" is basic data processing. A sales dashboard with totals, regional averages and trends is outside, as long as it does not recommend what to promote. A baseline forecast that simply predicts average daily sales is a "simple prediction system". A fixed rule your team wrote, such as "free shipping over €50", is also outside.

Other tools are likely inside. The guidelines describe AI-based recommenders that learn from large data sets, adapt to user behaviour in real time and personalise suggestions. Tools that generate text or images with large language models produce "content", which is one of the listed outputs.

Two cautions. First, the guidelines are not binding. They also say no automatic list of in-scope or out-of-scope systems is possible. You must judge each system by its architecture and function. Second, being an AI system does not bring heavy duties by itself. The guidelines note that most AI systems fall outside the Act's bans, high-risk rules and transparency duties. The Article 4 AI literacy duty does apply to any AI system you provide or deploy.

Best practice: keep an inventory of the software in your shop. For each tool, record a yes or no on Article 3(1) and one line of reasons. That note is the starting point for every other check: bans under Article 5, high-risk status under Article 6 and Annex III, and transparency under Article 50.
