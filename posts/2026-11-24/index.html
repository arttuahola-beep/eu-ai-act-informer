Tuesday 24 November 2026 Financial institutions

If your credit-scoring AI breaks the rules, fix it fast and say so

Takeaway: A bank may find that its own credit-scoring AI does not comply with the AI Act. If so, it must act at once. It must fix the system, withdraw it, disable it or recall it. This is Article 20 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Who this applies to. A bank that builds its own credit-scoring model is the model's provider (Art. 3(3)). Credit scoring of natural persons is high-risk under Annex III 5(b). Article 16(j) makes Article 20 one of the provider's duties.

The trigger is low. Article 20(1) applies when the provider "considers or has reason to consider" that the system is not in conformity. You do not need proof first. A credible warning sign is enough.

Who must be told. The provider must inform distributors. Where relevant, it must also inform deployers, the authorised representative and importers. If other group companies use the model, they are deployers. Tell them.

When there is a real risk. Some systems pose a risk to health, safety or fundamental rights (Art. 79(1)). If so, the provider must look into the cause straight away. Where relevant, it does this together with the deployer that reported the problem (Art. 20(2)). It must tell the market surveillance authority what is wrong and what it has done. For banks, that authority is in principle the financial supervisor (Art. 74(6)).

Timing. For Annex III systems, Article 20 applies from 2 December 2027. The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, set that date.

Penalty. Breaking provider duties can cost up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. The higher amount applies (Art. 99(4)(a)).

Best practice. Write a corrective-action playbook now. Link it to your Article 9 risk management and your Article 72 post-market monitoring. Decide who makes the call. Define what counts as "reason to consider." Name who notifies the supervisor. Test a switch to a fallback scorecard or manual underwriting. If you buy the model from a vendor, require the vendor by contract to warn you at once.
