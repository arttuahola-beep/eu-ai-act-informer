Marketplace recommenders: the DSA rules apply now, whatever the AI Act says

If your web shop is also a marketplace, your AI recommender already has binding rules. They come from the Digital Services Act (DSA), not the AI Act.

The AI Act does not treat product recommenders as high-risk. They are not listed in Annex III (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 6(2)). The AI Act also says it does not affect the DSA liability rules for intermediary services (Art. 2(5)). So the DSA is where the recommender duties sit today.

Who is caught? The DSA covers "online platforms". That means a hosting service that stores and shares content from users with the public (DSA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, Art. 3(i)). A marketplace with third-party sellers fits. A shop that sells only its own stock usually does not.

What counts as a recommender? Any fully or partly automated system that suggests or ranks items on the platform. Search result ranking is included (Art. 3(s)). Most AI ranking and "you may also like" tools meet this test.

The core duty is in Article 27. Your terms and conditions must explain the main parameters of each recommender. Use plain and intelligible language (Art. 27(1)). Say which criteria matter most. Say why they carry that weight (Art. 27(2)). If users can change the ranking, tell them how. If several options exist, users must be able to switch at any time. The switch must sit right where the ranked items appear (Art. 27(3)).

Two more points matter for retail. First, no ads based on profiling when you are reasonably certain the user is a minor (Art. 28(2)). Second, very large online platforms must offer at least one recommender option not based on profiling (Art. 38).

There is relief for small firms. Micro and small enterprises are exempt from these platform duties, unless designated as very large (Art. 19). The DSA has applied since 17 February 2024 (Art. 93(2)). Fines can reach 6% of annual worldwide turnover (Art. 52(3)).

Best practice: keep one register of your ranking tools. For each one, record the main parameters, the user options, and where the terms explain them. Review it whenever the model or its signals change.
