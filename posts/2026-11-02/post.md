If your credit-scoring AI causes a serious incident, the AI Act sets a reporting clock. The longest deadline is 15 days. Banks should plan for it now.

Article 73 puts the reporting duty on the provider of a high-risk AI system. A bank that builds its own credit-scoring model is that provider. The report goes to the market surveillance authority in the country where the incident happened. For banks, that is usually the financial supervisor (Article 74(6)).

What counts as a serious incident? Article 3(49) lists four outcomes. They are death or serious harm to health, serious disruption of critical infrastructure, serious harm to property or the environment, and a breach of EU law that protects fundamental rights. For a lender, the last one matters most. Think of a model that systematically discriminates against a protected group.

The deadlines are short. Report as soon as you find a causal link, or a reasonable likelihood of one. The outer limit is 15 days after you become aware (Article 73(2)). It drops to 2 days for a widespread infringement and 10 days for a death (Article 73(3)-(4)). You may file an incomplete first report and complete it later (Article 73(5)).

After reporting, the provider must investigate. That includes a risk assessment and corrective action (Article 73(6)). Do not change the model in a way that hides the cause before you tell the authority.

There is a possible narrowing. Article 73(9) covers providers already under EU rules with equivalent reporting duties. For them, only fundamental-rights breaches must be reported. Whether existing financial reporting regimes count as equivalent is not settled. Do not assume it.

Banks that buy a scoring tool have a duty too. Under Article 26(5), a deployer that spots a serious incident must inform the provider immediately. It must then inform the importer or distributor and the authority. The financial-institution shortcut in Article 26(5) is tied to the monitoring duty. Treat incident escalation as its own step.

The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, did not change Article 73. It moved the start date. For Annex III systems such as credit scoring, it applies from 2 December 2027.

Practical step: add an AI incident path to your existing incident process. Name who decides on causality. Name who files. Keep the 2, 10 and 15 day limits visible. The Commission consulted on draft guidance and a reporting template in September 2025; check for the final version.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 3(49), 26(5), 73 and 74(6); Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.
