The marking duty in Article 50(2) falls on the provider of the AI tool, not on the retailer using it. Retailers should still check that their tools meet it before 2 December 2026.

Article 50(2) says providers of generative AI systems must mark synthetic image, audio, video and text output in a machine-readable way. The output must be detectable as AI-generated. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 gives a short grace period. It applies only to systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026. Those providers must comply by 2 December 2026. Systems placed on the market from 2 August 2026 had no grace period.

A retailer that only uses a third-party tool for product photos or descriptions is a deployer. The retailer does not have to build the marking. A retailer that builds its own tool and offers it under its own name is a provider. That retailer carries the duty itself.

Deployers still have their own duty in Article 50(4). They must clearly label a deepfake. A deepfake is AI-generated image, audio or video that resembles existing persons, objects, places or events and would falsely appear authentic. Per the Commission, a deployer cannot rely on the provider's hidden machine-readable mark. The label must be visible or audible, and shown at first exposure at the latest. A realistic AI-made person wearing a product could fall under this. The Commission's three tests decide each case.

Article 50(4) also covers AI-generated text, but only text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Ordinary product descriptions are unlikely to fall under it. Text that has had real human review or editorial control does not need the label either. A spell-check does not count as review.

What to do now:
- List every AI tool used for product images, video and copy.
- Ask each vendor in writing how it marks output and when it started to do so.
- Ask whether the vendor has signed the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated content. Signing is voluntary. Providers outside the code may face more information requests.
- Write a rule for when a realistic AI image gets a visible label.

The fine ceiling for breaching Article 50 duties is EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover, whichever is higher (Art. 99(4)). Consumer-protection rules on misleading images still apply separately.
