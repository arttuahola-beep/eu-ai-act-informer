If your bank builds its own credit-scoring model, the technical documentation is not optional paperwork. Under Article 11 of the AI Act, it must exist before the model goes live. It must also be kept up to date.

**Why it applies.** Credit scoring of natural persons is high-risk under Annex III point 5(b). A bank that develops a model and puts it into service under its own name is the provider (Art. 3(3)). Article 11 is a provider duty. For Annex III systems it applies from 2 December 2027, after the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).

**What the file must do.** Article 11(1) says the documentation must show that the system meets the high-risk requirements. It must give authorities the information in a clear and comprehensive form. Annex IV sets the minimum contents. In short:

**General description:** intended purpose, provider name, version, links to other software, user interface and instructions for use (Annex IV point 1).

**How it was built:** methods, any third-party pre-trained tools, design logic, key choices and trade-offs, what it optimises for, architecture and compute (point 2(a)-(c)).

**Data:** datasheets, provenance, and how data was selected, labelled and cleaned (point 2(d)).

Human oversight measures under Article 14 (point 2(e)).

**Testing:** validation and test data, accuracy and robustness metrics, potentially discriminatory impacts, and dated, signed test reports (point 2(g)).

Cybersecurity measures (point 2(h)).

**Limits:** accuracy for specific groups of persons, and foreseeable risks to fundamental rights and of discrimination (point 3).

**Also:** the Article 9 risk management system (point 5), lifecycle changes (point 6), standards applied (point 7), the EU declaration of conformity (point 8) and the Article 72 post-market monitoring plan (point 9).


**How long to keep it.** Article 18(1) sets ten years after the system is placed on the market or put into service. Article 18(3) adds a shortcut for financial institutions. They keep the technical documentation as part of the documentation they already hold under EU financial services law. So it can sit with your existing model files. It does not need a separate archive.

**Smaller firms.** As amended by the Omnibus, Article 11(1) lets SMEs, start-ups and small mid-caps (SMCs) provide the Annex IV items in a simplified way. The Commission must publish a simplified form for this. A firm that takes this route must use that form.

**Best practice.** Map your current model documentation against Annex IV now. Mark each gap. Most banks already document development, validation and monitoring. The likely gaps are the AI Act items: accuracy by group, discriminatory impacts, human-oversight design, cybersecurity against AI-specific attacks (Art. 15(5)) and the post-market monitoring plan. Give each gap an owner well before 2 December 2027. This file also feeds the Annex VI internal check and the EU declaration of conformity.
