Monday 16 November 2026 Financial institutions

Credit-scoring AI needs a living risk management system (Article 9)

Takeaway: If your bank builds its own credit-scoring model, you need a risk management system for it. This is not a one-off risk review. It is a living process that runs for the model's whole life.

Why it applies. AI that scores the creditworthiness of individuals is high-risk under Annex III point 5(b). A bank that builds the model and uses it under its own name is the provider (Art. 3(3)). Providers must make sure their system meets Article 9 (Art. 16(a)).

What Article 9 asks for:

Set it up and keep it running. The system must be established, implemented, documented and maintained (Art. 9(1)).

Treat it as a cycle. It is a continuous, iterative process across the whole lifecycle. It needs regular, systematic review and updating (Art. 9(2)).

Four steps. Identify and analyse known and reasonably foreseeable risks to health, safety or fundamental rights. Estimate and evaluate risks under intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse. Evaluate new risks found through post-market monitoring under Art. 72. Then adopt targeted measures (Art. 9(2)(a)-(d)).

Accept only acceptable leftovers. After the measures, the residual risk of each hazard, and overall, must be judged acceptable (Art. 9(5)).

Prefer design fixes. First remove or reduce risk through design and development, as far as technically feasible. Then add mitigation and controls. Then give deployers information under Art. 13 and, where needed, training (Art. 9(5)).

Test before go-live. Testing must happen during development as appropriate, and in any event before the model is placed on the market or put into service. Use metrics and probabilistic thresholds defined in advance (Art. 9(6) and 9(8)). Real-world testing under Art. 60 is allowed (Art. 9(7)).

Think about vulnerable people. Consider whether the model is likely to harm people under 18 or other vulnerable groups (Art. 9(9)).

What this means for a lender. For credit scoring, the main risk is to fundamental rights. Think unfair refusals, biased outcomes for protected groups, or a model that drifts as the economy changes. Each of these should be a named hazard, with an owner, a test and a threshold.

The good news. Banks already run risk management under EU financial services law. Article 9(10) lets you fold the AI Act steps into those existing procedures. You do not need a parallel framework. But note: the QMS shortcut in Art. 17(4) does not cover risk management. So Article 9 itself must still be met.

Timing. Under the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), Annex III high-risk duties apply from 2 December 2027.

Practical step. Map your current model risk management cycle against Art. 9(2)(a)-(d). Look for gaps on fundamental-rights risks, misuse scenarios and pre-set test thresholds. Close them in 2027 before the deadline.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 3(3), 9, 13, 16(a), 17(4), 60, 72 and Annex III 5(b); Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.
