Built your own credit-scoring AI? Keep watching it after go-live

If you build your own credit-scoring AI, your duties do not stop at go-live. Article 72 of the AI Act requires you to keep monitoring it for its whole life.

Who this hits. Credit scoring and creditworthiness checks of natural persons are high-risk (Annex III, point 5(b)). If a bank builds the model and uses it under its own name, the bank is the provider (Art. 3(3)). Article 72 is a provider duty.

What the law asks.

- Set up a post-market monitoring system and document it. It must fit the technology and its risks (Art. 72(1)).

- Collect, record and analyse data on how the system performs over its whole life. The data can come from deployers or other sources (Art. 72(2)).

- Use that data to check that the system still meets the high-risk requirements in Articles 8 to 15 (Art. 72(2)).

- Where relevant, look at how it interacts with other AI systems (Art. 72(2)).

- Base all of this on a written monitoring plan. The plan is part of the Annex IV technical documentation (Art. 72(3)).

What the Omnibus changed. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 removed the Commission's power to set a mandatory plan template by implementing act. Instead, the Commission must publish guidance with a voluntary template by 2 September 2027 (Art. 72(3) as amended; Omnibus Recital 41). So you can build a plan that fits your organisation.

The bank shortcut. Financial institutions covered by EU financial-services governance rules may fold these elements into systems and plans they already run. The condition is that it gives an equivalent level of protection (Art. 72(4), second subparagraph). Your existing model-risk monitoring is the natural home.

When. Under the Omnibus, the Annex III high-risk duties apply from 2 December 2027.

Why it matters. Monitoring feeds other duties. Its findings go back into the risk management process (Art. 9(2)(c)). A real problem can lead to corrective action (Art. 20) or a serious incident report (Art. 73).

Practical step. Extend your current model monitoring to cover AI Act points. Track accuracy drift, outcomes across groups, how often human reviewers override the model, complaints and incidents. Write it up as the plan and name an owner.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Arts. 3(3), 9(2)(c), 20, 72, 73, Annex III(5)(b), Annex IV; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Recital 41.
